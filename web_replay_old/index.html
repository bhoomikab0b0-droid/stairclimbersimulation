      Across 30 random staircases

      Each staircase had 5 steps with riser heights of 140–200 mm, tread depths of 325–375 mm and widths of 0.75–1.30 m, all randomized. Results from python -m analysis.batch --runs 30.

      How the robot decides

      Perceive

      • 160-beam LiDAR fan with ±4 mm range noise, levelled using the IMU.
      • Each scan is split into treads and risers by local line fitting.
      • A Kalman filter fuses 12 scans into riser height, tread depth and riser position.
      • Side rangefinders measure stair width; odometry tracks travel.

      Decide

      • A 10-state machine runs the gait: scan, align, approach, clean, then front and rear climb phases.
      • A gait library picks LOW / STANDARD / HIGH from the riser height.
      • It refuses risers over 220 mm or taller than the sensor, treads under 320 mm, and stairs the frame cannot span.
      • A tilt watchdog and per-state timeouts trigger SAFE_ABORT.

      Act

      • PID loops drive each rack, the frame width and the wheels.
      • While the rear units lift, an IMU pitch PID trims the front rack to keep the body level.
      • A heading-hold PID keeps both side units parallel to the walls.
      • The mop head sweeps each tread in rows; touch/stall detection absorbs odometry drift.

      Simplifications: omni-wheels use reduced sideways friction instead of individual rollers; the rear rack pad slides rather than grips; cleaning is counted geometrically where the lowered head passes; ascent only.
